<Part kind="answer">

Magma rises because it's lighter than the solid rock around it, and the gas inside it forms bubbles that grow as it rises, pushing it out with tremendous force.

In some places, often where the giant plates of Earth's surface meet or pull apart, rock tens of kilometers down gets hot enough to melt. The melted rock, called **magma**, is lighter than the solid rock around it, so it slowly rises, like a bubble in honey, and collects in a **magma chamber**.

Magma contains dissolved gases, mostly water vapor and carbon dioxide. Deep down, the huge weight of rock above keeps them squeezed in. But as magma rises toward the surface, the pressure drops, and the gas comes out as bubbles. The bubbles grow, the magma gets frothy and lighter, and it rises faster still. Eventually it bursts out of the ground: an **eruption**.

How violent an eruption is depends on the magma:

- **Runny magma**, like at the volcanoes of Hawaii, lets gas bubbles escape easily. It pours out as rivers of lava.
- **Thick, sticky magma** traps the gas until the pressure becomes huge. Then it explodes, blasting rock into ash, as when Mount St Helens erupted in 1980.

Magma that reaches the surface is called **lava**.

</Part>

<Myth claim="Lava is the most dangerous part of a volcano.">

Lava usually moves slowly enough to walk away from. The real dangers are fast avalanches of scorching gas and ash, called *pyroclastic flows*, falling ash, and mudflows that race down valleys. Scientists watch volcanoes closely so people can leave in time.

</Myth>

<TryIt title="a fizzy eruption" need={['A big bottle of diet cola', 'A pack of mint candies (Mentos)', 'A sheet of paper rolled into a tube', 'A big outdoor space and an adult']} safety="Do this outdoors with an adult, wear old clothes, and step back quickly once the candies drop in.">

1. Stand the bottle on flat ground outdoors and open it.
2. Roll the paper into a tube the width of a candy, and load about five candies into it.
3. Hold the tube over the bottle's mouth and let all the candies drop in at once. Step back!
4. The candies' bumpy surface helps the dissolved gas form bubbles very fast, and the foam shoots up. Rising magma does something similar when its gas comes out.

</TryIt>
