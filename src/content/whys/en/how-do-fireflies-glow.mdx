<Part kind="answer">

Fireflies make light with a chemical reaction in their tails, called bioluminescence. They switch it on and off by letting oxygen in.

Making light from chemistry is called **bioluminescence** ("living light"). In a firefly's tail is a special light organ packed with luciferin and luciferase. When oxygen reaches them, they react and give off a flash of yellow-green light.

Most lights, like old light bulbs, waste lots of energy as heat. A firefly's light is **cold light**: the reaction makes almost no heat, which is lucky, or the firefly would cook itself!

To flash, a firefly controls how much oxygen reaches its light organ, switching the light on and off in a split second.

Why flash at all? To **talk**. Male fireflies fly around flashing a pattern, and females on the ground answer with their own flash. Every kind of firefly has its own code: the number of flashes, their length and the gaps between them. Some sneaky fireflies even copy other species' codes to lure them in... and eat them!

</Part>

<Myth claim="Fireflies are a kind of fly.">

Despite the name, fireflies are **beetles**, related to ladybirds. They have hard wing covers, which they lift to fly, just like other beetles.

</Myth>

<TryIt title="warm light, cool light" need={['Two glow sticks', 'A cup of warm tap water', 'A cup of ice water', 'A dark room']}>

1. Snap both glow sticks to start them glowing. Glow sticks use a chemical reaction too, a bit like a firefly.
2. Put one in the warm water and the other in the ice water.
3. Wait two minutes, then turn off the lights and compare.
4. The warm one glows brighter: heat speeds up the chemical reaction. That's why fireflies flash faster on warm nights!

</TryIt>
