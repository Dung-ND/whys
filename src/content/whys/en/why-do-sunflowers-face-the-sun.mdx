<Part kind="answer">

Young sunflowers follow the Sun by growing faster on one side of their stem: the east side during the day and the west side at night. Grown-up flowers stop moving and face east.

Plants can't walk or wave, but they can **grow unevenly**. If one side of a stem grows a little faster than the other, the stem bends.

During the day, the **east** side of a young sunflower's stem grows faster, so the flower bends steadily west, tracking the Sun across the sky. At night, the **west** side grows faster, and by dawn the flower faces east again, ready for sunrise.

How does it know when? Sunflowers have an **internal clock**, like the one that makes you sleepy at bedtime. In experiments, plants kept in steady light carried on swinging back and forth for a few days.

When the sunflower is fully grown and its flower opens, the stem stops growing and it stops moving. Almost all grown sunflowers end up facing **east**. Scientists discovered why: east-facing flowers warm up faster in the morning sun, and bees love warm flowers.

</Part>

<Myth claim="Sunflowers in full bloom follow the Sun all day.">

Only young sunflowers track the Sun. Once a sunflower blooms, its stem stops growing and the flower stays put, usually facing east. Look at a sunflower field in the evening: most faces point away from the setting Sun!

</Myth>

<TryIt title="a plant that bends to the light" need={['A bean or sunflower seedling in a pot (or sprout a dried bean in damp cotton wool)', 'A sunny windowsill', 'A notebook to draw in']}>

1. Put the seedling on a sunny windowsill.
2. Each morning for a few days, draw which way it leans.
3. Then turn the pot halfway around, so it leans away from the window.
4. Within a day or two, it bends back toward the light, by growing faster on its shady side!

</TryIt>
